The signs

  • Nobody owns the numbers. Metrics come from whoever built the dashboard, and leadership meetings start with an argument about which figure is right.
  • Your data people report to someone who is not a data leader. A strong engineer or analyst reporting to the CTO or CFO often lacks someone who can set direction, review their work, and represent data in leadership decisions.
  • A big decision is due. A warehouse migration, a platform contract, a first data hire, or a board question about AI. Each is hard to reverse, and each is easy to make on a vendor's advice alone.
  • Regulation is arriving. An audit, a new market, a healthcare or payments customer, or a young player base. Governance becomes a requirement rather than a nice-to-have.
  • The warehouse bill is growing faster than the business, and nobody can explain why.

What a fractional head of data does

The same things a full-time one does, at a smaller scope. They own the data roadmap and the budget line. They set standards for how data is modeled, tested, and governed. They help hire and grow the team, including writing the first job descriptions and interviewing. They sit in leadership meetings as the person accountable for the numbers. And they make the platform decisions, with a written rationale the company keeps.

What they should not do is become a very expensive individual contributor. If most of their time goes into building pipelines, you need an engineer, not a leader.

How to know it is working

By the end of the first quarter, leadership meetings should argue about what to do with the numbers, not about which number is right. The team should know what it is building and why. The warehouse bill should have an owner and a trend line. And there should be a written record of every major decision, so that none of it depends on one person remembering.

How it ends

A good fractional engagement is designed to end. Most end with a full-time hire, either a head of data the fractional leader helped recruit, or a senior member of the team grown into the role. The handoff includes the roadmap, the decision record, and a period of overlap. The other good ending is that the company decides it does not need a full-time data executive yet and moves to lighter advisory support. Either way, the test is that the company is stronger without you than it was before you arrived.
