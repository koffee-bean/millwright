03  Work with me

Three ways in.

I work best on problems where the platform is the bottleneck: the models are ready but nobody trusts the data, the cloud bill has no owner, or the AI roadmap needs a foundation before it needs a vendor. Pick the shape that matches how urgent it is.

Platform diagnostic

Two weeks, fixed fee. I read your architecture, your cost data, and your governance as it actually runs, then hand you a written findings memo with a fix-first order. Good when a number moved and nobody can say why, or before you sign a large vendor contract.

Warehouse and cluster spend review. Catalog and lineage gap analysis. A second opinion on an AI analytics vendor shortlist.

Build engagement

Four to eight weeks, defined deliverable. I design it, build it with your engineers, document it, and leave it running in your accounts. Your team owns it on day one.

Metadata catalog rollout. FinOps dashboards and workload policies. A semantic layer with governed metrics. A model-to-production pipeline with CI gates.

Ongoing advisory

Monthly, on call. A senior data and AI platform leader in your leadership meetings, without the hire. Roadmaps, vendor negotiations, executive readouts, and hiring plans. Available as a fractional head of data platforms for teams that need one or two days a week.

Build-versus-buy calls on AI tooling. Org design for the first governance and MLOps functions. Reviewing the data platform line in the budget.

Speaking and workshops

Conference talks, panels, and half-day internal workshops on data governance, AI platform architecture, and platform economics. Recent stages: Data Innovation Summit Stockholm and Big Data LDN.

Running one data quality standard across teams that ship very different products. FinOps for Snowflake and Databricks estates.

Across every engagement

The same four areas of expertise show up whichever shape you choose.

AI-ready data platform

Platform-neutral target states that avoid single-vendor lock-in: open table formats, a semantic layer, policy enforcement, and lineage. Structured vendor evaluations with real criteria and a decision gate.

Trino or DuckDB over Iceberg, Cube and MetricFlow, OPA, OpenLineage, DataHub. Per-user identity propagation instead of shared service principals.

Governance for regulated data

Operating models with real owners and stewards, not a policy deck. Tokenization, redaction, fine-grained access, and audit-ready logging for data that regulators care about.

HIPAA and PHI, PCI DSS, SOX, GDPR and CCPA data-subject rights, COPPA and age-gating for player safety.

Cost and FinOps

Warehouse and cluster economics: workload policies, auto-suspend and auto-scale tuning, tagging, budgets, and unit economics that leadership can read. Vendor escalation when the bill is the vendor's fault.

Surfaced a Databricks bug driving runaway S3 cost from Delta metadata, and drove it to a fix and credits.

MLOps and agentic analytics

The path from experimentation to production: feature pipelines, model registry, scheduled scoring, CI/CD gates. Agents grounded in governed metrics, with human review where it matters.

AI listening agents over customer service interactions. An AI pull request reviewer enforced as a CI merge gate on a data engineering monorepo.
04  How I work

Ground rules.

I have sat on the buying side of enough consulting engagements to know what goes wrong. These are the terms I hold myself to.

  • Scope in writing

    After the intro call you get a one-page scope: the problem, what done looks like, the timeline, and the price. If it changes, the page changes first.

  • Work in the open

    You see the work as it happens, in your repos and your accounts, with a short written update at the end of every week. No reveal at the end.

  • Your team owns it

    Everything I build is documented and runs in your infrastructure under your credentials. When I leave, nothing leaves with me.

  • No vendor commissions

    I recommend tools I have run in production, and I take no referral fees from any of them. When I say buy, it is because I would buy it.

  • Honest fit

    If your problem is not one I have solved before, I will say so on the intro call and point you to someone who has.

millwright/ˈmɪl.raɪt/noun

The craftsman who designed, built, and kept the machinery of a mill, so that the whole works turned. The miller ground the flour. The millwright made sure he could.

Origin: Middle English, from mill + wright, a maker or builder, from Old English wyrhta, worker.
